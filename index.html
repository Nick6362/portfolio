About

I am a PhD student in Biostatistics at the University of South Carolina. My work sits at the intersection of biostatistics, data science, and public health, with an emphasis on statistical and computational methods for complex health data.

My current research focuses on wearable sensor data, time-use behavior, missing-data methods, time-series forecasting, and reproducible statistical software. As a Graduate Research Assistant in Exercise Science, I develop R workflows that integrate accelerometry with time-use diaries, strengthen data quality, and support collaborative behavioral research.

Research interests

Wearable and time-use data

Integrating accelerometry, heart-rate, and diary data to characterize movement, sleep, and daily behavior.

Missing data and forecasting

Studying how imputation choices affect time-series inference and forecasting with ARIMA and LSTM models.

Statistical learning

Building reproducible models and workflows that translate biomedical data into clear, defensible evidence.
